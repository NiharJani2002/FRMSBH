EXTENSION #1 -- Fed-Tree: Federated Robust Cardinality Across K Sharded Nodes
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Real data: the true join-day of all 505 S&P500 tickers in the feed
(476 present at inception, ~29 real later index additions). Tickers are sharded
across K nodes by (tickerId mod K) -- disjoint partitions, exactly how a sharded
messaging/monitoring system already looks. This is a setting the paper's own
single-sketch model cannot be applied to at all without first centralizing every
shard's raw keys, which defeats the point of sharding.

Fed-Tree gives EVERY node the full target epsilon (valid: disjoint data, classical
parallel composition). 'Naive-Split' instead divides epsilon by K, as an engineer
unaware of parallel composition might reflexively do. Everything else (partition,
sampling rate p=0.5) is identical between the two columns.

Each K is run over 40 independent seeds (Agarwal et al., NeurIPS
2021: point estimates from few runs are unreliable, report interval estimates). The
headline ratio is computed as mean(naive_err)/mean(fed_err) -- a RATIO OF MEANS, not a
mean of per-seed ratios. That distinction matters here: a ratio of two noisy
quantities is itself a heavy-tailed, unstable statistic whenever the denominator can
land near zero (classical ratio-estimator bias, Cochran, 'Sampling Techniques,' 1977),
which is exactly what made the previous single-seed, mean-of-ratios version of this
table non-monotonic. Averaging numerator and denominator SEPARATELY first, then
dividing, is the standard fix.

Proposition being tested: misapplying composition (Naive-Split) costs a full FACTOR
OF K in noise standard deviation relative to Fed-Tree's correct calibration -- watch
the ratio-of-means column trend upward with K, now averaged over 40 seeds per row
instead of read off a single draw.

Paper comparison: Theorem 2.4 reports O(eps^-2 * log^1.5(T) * log(T/delta)) bits for a
SINGLE node and offers no federated mode at all. Fed-Tree is the first mechanism in
this line of work with any adaptively-robust guarantee for a sharded deployment; the
cost of getting the sharding math wrong (Naive-Split) is what is newly quantified here.
